SEA Boom Radar

Can public data tell which Silicon Valley trends will become Southeast Asia's next venture booms, and which are hype? We backtested two methods on 19 historical cases. The composite scores failed. One timing signal held up.

The question

Many of SEA's biggest venture booms began as San Francisco ideas. So did a lot of the hype.

Grab and Gojek followed Uber, GoPay and GCash followed Venmo and Square, and Atome and Kredivo followed Affirm. But daily deals, coworking, e-scooters, cloud kitchens, quick commerce, NFTs, the metaverse, play-to-earn and Clubhouse also started in SF, and each was loudly predicted to sweep the region. The useful question for a Singapore fund is narrow: while a wave is still hot in SF, can you tell which kind it is?

We assembled search interest for the SF Bay Area and six SEA markets (Google Trends), Hacker News discussion, Y Combinator batches, GitHub activity, research output (OpenAlex), Wikipedia pageviews in English and five SEA languages, and World Bank readiness data. Each count is converted to a share of its platform's total, so platform growth doesn't masquerade as a trend.

Pre-registered result

The composite scores did not separate booms from hype

Two methods were fixed before the backtest. Method A scores convergence: are attention, founders, developers and research rising together, steadily, without a spike? Method B uses time-series statistics plus a machine-learning model trained on 1,865 YC theme-years. Both were scored only inside each wave's decision window (the four years before a boom reached SEA, or the run-up to a fad's peak) using data available at that moment.

AUC = probability a random boom-year scores above a random hype-year. 0.5 is a coin flip. Bars show 95% intervals from resampling whole sectors.

What this means

Neither method, nor their average, is an investment signal as built. Every interval crosses 0.5, and at a fixed threshold the scores flagged 10 of 12 fads. An earlier run of this study reported 0.73 for Method A. That number came from an evaluation-window bug and is retracted.

What held up

Hype hits SF and SEA in the same month. Real booms arrive later.

Breaking the scores into parts showed that one ingredient did nearly all of the work: timing between markets. A media-driven wave moves search interest in San Francisco and Jakarta, Manila and Ho Chi Minh City at the same time, because the news cycle is global. A real adoption wave needs local operators, licences and payment rails, so SEA follows months later.

As a single signal, low synchronisation separated booms from fads with an AUC of 0.77 (permutation p = 0.03). We found it after the main test failed and picked it from six components, so the honest reading is a strong hypothesis, not a proven edge. The interval runs from about 0.49 to 1.0 with only 13 sectors.

Two more findings

Crowd signals point the wrong way

  • Following the buzz loses. Ranking waves by how fast SF attention is growing scored an AUC of 0.37, worse than a coin flip. At decision time, the fads were simply louder than the future booms.
  • Founders chase the same waves. The model trained to predict whether YC founders keep building a theme scored 0.27 against SEA outcomes. Inverted, that's 0.73. Founder momentum peaked in NFTs, the metaverse and play-to-earn, not in BNPL or digital banking.
  • Shape tells you only afterwards. Two to three years after the peak, booms kept 63% of peak SF interest and fads kept 18%. Fads are 3.5× spikier. By the time this is visible, the investment decision has passed.

These reads rest on one signal and thin data.

Method and limits

How to check this

  • No look-ahead. Each year's score uses only data up to that year. The ML model is retrained every year on examples whose outcome was already known. All features are ratios or growth rates, so Google Trends' 0–100 rescaling can't leak the future.
  • Labels fixed in advance with a one-line rationale each. Six disputed sectors (crypto exchanges, telemedicine, edtech, insurtech, P2P lending, B2B SaaS) are excluded.
  • Small sample. 19 events is enough to reject a strong claim and to suggest a hypothesis, not to estimate an accuracy.
  • Free data only. No deal-level funding data. YC is mostly B2B, so the founder signal is thin for consumer sectors.
  • Blind spot by design: SEA-native booms with no SF precedent (super-apps, live and social commerce).

Next step: fix a synchronisation-led score today and score it on waves that resolve after October 2026. That is the only test free of hindsight. The code, data pipeline and all six notebooks are reproducible from public sources.
